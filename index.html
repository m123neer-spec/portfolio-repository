PROJECT 03AERODYNAMICS / XFLR5GRADUATION PROJECT

Airfoil thickness
optimization

Compared symmetric NACA profiles by reducing the thickness-to-chord ratio from 12% to 10% while holding the chord constant. Geometry, flow fields and aerodynamic polars were evaluated under matched conditions.

120 mmChord3.53%CD reduction at α = 0°100kReynolds number
Explore the complete study +
Problem

Reduce aerodynamic drag by changing one controlled geometric variable: symmetric airfoil thickness.

Method

Generate 12%, 11.5%, 11%, 10.5% and 10% profiles; keep chord, camber and numerical conditions fixed; compare mesh, flow fields and polars.

Result

At α = 0°, CD changed from 0.00481 to 0.00464 after matching Reynolds number and transition settings.

Key engineering correction

An early comparison used inconsistent transition settings. I identified the mismatch and repeated the comparison at Re = 100,000, Mach = 0 and Ncrit = 9, making the baseline and optimized results directly comparable.

PROJECT 04CFD / ANSYSCOURSEWORK

Hot and cold
water mixing

Built a faucet-flow model with two velocity inlets and a pressure outlet. The simulation visualized velocity reversal after a sudden expansion, streamline development and temperature mixing.

3,493Nodes12,489Elements
View CFD setup and results +

Geometry

Main body diameter: 30 mm. Each inlet diameter: 15 mm. Bend radius: 50 mm.

Boundary conditions

Cold inlet: 0.5 m/s and 290 K. Hot inlet: 0.4 m/s and 350 K. The outlet was a pressure boundary, with wall and symmetry conditions.

Flow interpretation

The vector field revealed recirculation after the sudden expansion. Farther downstream, the reversed-flow magnitude decreased and the main velocity field realigned.

How to read these figures

Together, the mesh, vector and temperature plots document model setup and the predicted flow/mixing pattern. The figures are simulation outputs and should be interpreted alongside mesh-quality and validation checks.

Line and operating point

Engineering review before selection

The workbook iteration converges to f ≈ 0.10371 using a turbulent-flow approximation, while Re ≈ 279 is laminar. For fully developed laminar flow, the Darcy friction factor is 64/Re ≈ 0.229. The reported head and power should therefore be rechecked with a laminar model, signed elevation change, and a pump curve corrected for viscous castor oil before equipment is selected.
